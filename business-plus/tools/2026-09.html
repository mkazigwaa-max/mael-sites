Your AI Assistant Just Got the Keys to Your Other Software

The story of September isn’t a smarter chatbot. It’s that the assistant in your inbox can now open your books, your customer list and your email tool, and that the bill for AI is quietly changing shape.

The Big One

The assistant in your inbox can now read your books

On September 15, Google switched on a new ability for Gemini, the assistant built into Gmail, Docs and Sheets. It can now reach into seven outside tools: QuickBooks, Mailchimp, HubSpot, Salesforce, Asana, Monday and Atlassian. You connect a tool once, and from then on you can ask questions across it from inside Google. “Who owes me money past 30 days, and draft each of them a reminder” used to be three tabs and twenty minutes. Now it’s one question.

Two things to know. First, it’s included in the Google Workspace plans most small teams already pay for, from Business Standard up, and in the consumer Google AI Pro and Ultra subscriptions. There’s no add-on to buy. Second, it’s switched on by default. Every person in your company with Gemini can connect your QuickBooks to their assistant today, unless an administrator turns it off. Whether that’s a gift or a problem depends on who’s on your team.

Our view: this is the most useful thing to happen to small-business AI this year, because it’s the first time the assistant can see the numbers that actually run your company instead of the emails about them. It’s also the first time you need to decide who gets that view. Spend ten minutes this month deciding, before someone decides for you by clicking Connect.

Mini Briefs

Microsoft’s Copilot bill is about to have a meter on it

On September 25 Microsoft rebuilt Copilot into one app with Word, Excel and PowerPoint inside it, plus two new pieces: Code, which builds small tools for you, and Autopilot, an agent that works on a goal without being asked each time. Most of that is still in preview. The quieter change matters more. From November 2, a new Copilot Business license bought through a Microsoft reseller comes with pay-as-you-go billing switched on. The flat $21 a person a month becomes the floor, and the newer features draw down credits on top, under a preset monthly cap the administrator can raise, lower or remove. If you’re buying Copilot this fall, know who approves the overage and set that cap on purpose.

The engines got stronger and cheaper. Again.

OpenAI released GPT-6 Astra in early September and calls it the most capable model it has ever broadly deployed. Anthropic followed on September 22 with Claude Opus 5.5, cut its list prices by 20 percent, and says it costs about 40 percent less to run on typical work once you count the efficiency gains. Those are the companies’ own numbers. The lesson is the one we wrote in August, one step further along: the tools you already use keep getting smarter at the same or lower price. Nobody should be paying a premium for “the AI version” of anything this year.

You can now talk to your inbox

On September 3 Google launched Gmail Live and Docs Live on the Gmail and Docs phone apps. You talk, and Gemini searches your inbox, reads you the thread that matters, or drafts a document from what you said. It’s built for the owner in the truck between jobs. The catch: it’s for the consumer Google AI plans right now (Gmail Live from the $4.99 Plus tier, Docs Live from the $19.99 Pro tier), and Google says it’s “coming soon” for Workspace business accounts. If your company email runs on Workspace, you’re waiting.

Tool of the Month

Meta One, the Essential business plan

Meta’s new subscription for businesses on Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp. The Essential plan gets you a verified badge, impersonation protection, and more use of Meta Business Agent, the AI that answers customers on WhatsApp around the clock and hands the conversation to a person when it should. For a business whose customers message rather than call, it’s the cheapest after-hours receptionist on the market.

Cost: $14.99 a month, per account. The higher tiers, from $49.99, buy more of the agent.

The 15-minute Win

Connect your assistant to one tool and ask it three questions.

  1. Pick the system you dig through most: QuickBooks, HubSpot or Mailchimp.
  2. Connect it to the assistant you already have. In Google Workspace, open the Workspace Marketplace, choose Integrations, find the tool and click Connect. In ChatGPT it’s Settings, then Connectors, and QuickBooks and Mailchimp work on the free plan. In Claude it’s Settings, then Connectors, on the paid plans.
  3. Ask the three questions you always go hunting for. “Who hasn’t paid me this month?” “Which customers haven’t heard from us since June?” “What did we spend on subcontractors last quarter?”

If the answers are right and took a minute, you’ve found where the time goes. If they’re wrong, you’ve learned that before it cost you anything.
